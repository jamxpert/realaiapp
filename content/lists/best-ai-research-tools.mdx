Normal chatbots can make up facts. These research tools show their sources, so you can check every answer.

## Quick comparison

<ComparisonTable tools={["perplexity","notebooklm","consensus","elicit"]} />

## 1. Perplexity: for quick answers from the web with sources

<ToolCard tool="perplexity" />

Ask any question and Perplexity searches the web and answers with links to its sources. A better starting point than a normal search.

## 2. NotebookLM: for studying your own documents

<ToolCard tool="notebooklm" />

Upload PDFs, notes or links and ask questions. NotebookLM answers only from your sources and shows exactly where each answer came from.

## 3. Consensus: for what scientific studies say

<ToolCard tool="consensus" />

Ask a yes-or-no question like "does caffeine improve memory?" and Consensus shows what published studies found.

## 4. Elicit: for literature reviews

<ToolCard tool="elicit" />

Elicit finds relevant papers and puts their main findings in a table, which saves days on a literature review.

<Callout type="warning">
Always open the sources and read them yourself. AI tools can still misread or summarise a source wrongly.
</Callout>

## Which one should you choose?

- **General questions:** Perplexity.
- **Your own notes, books or PDFs:** NotebookLM.
- **Academic and scientific research:** Consensus for quick answers, Elicit for full literature reviews.
